\section{Introduction}
These instructions give you guidelines for preparing papers for Jurnal Ilmu Komputer dan Informasi. Use this document as a template if you are using Microsoft Word or \LaTeX. Please use this document as a “template” to prepare your manuscript.

The manuscript is written with Times New Roman font size 10, single-spaced, left and right alligned, one one-sided pages and on A4 paper (210 mm x 297 mm) with the upper margin of 3 cm, lower margin 3 cm, left and right 3 and 3 cm based on odd and even pages. The manuscript including the graphic contents and tables should be between 6 to 15 pages. The manuscript is written in English. The Standard English grammar must be observed. The title of the article should be brief and informative and it should not exceed 20 words. The keywords are written after the abstract.

The first letter of headings is capitalized and headings are numbered in Arabic numerals. The organization of the manuscript includes Introduction, Methodology, Results and Analysis, Conclusion and References. Acknowledgement (if any) is written after Conclusion and before References and not numbered. The use of subheadings is discouraged. 

The use of abbreviation is permitted, but the abbreviation must be written in full and complete when it is mentioned for the first time and it should be written between parentheses. Terms/foreign words or regional words should be written in italics. Notations should be brief and clear and written according to the standardized writing style. Symbols/signs should be clear and distinguishable, such as the use of number 1 and letter l (also number 0 and leter O). In this manuscript doesn’t allow to use bullet and numbering. At the end of this paper both of the colomns should be in balance. You also have to activate widow or orphan control in order to ensure that there are no single line of sentence at the end of column section.     

\section{Tables}

There is one single space line between the table and the paragraph. The table is is placed immediately after it is referred to in the text. The frame of the table uses 1 font-size line. If the title in each table column is long and complex, the columns are numbered and the notes are given below the table.

\section{Citation}
Citation in the text should be written using Arabic numbers and put in order in accordance to what they refer to in the text. Numbers should be written in square brackets such as ``... Lafferty et al. \cite{Lafferty:2001:CRF:645530.655813} ...'' Citation should be written one space away from the words after commas or periods and before colons (:), semicolons (;), and question marks (?). If they are located at the end of a sentence, citations should be put before periods such as ``... from the most recent work by Kr\"{a}henb\"{u}hl and Koltun \cite{NIPS2011_4296}.'' If there are several citations, they should be written in a sorted and compressed form such as ``... by several research works \cite{Lafferty:2001:CRF:645530.655813,10.1109/TPAMI.2011.231,article.ChenPKMY14,article.farabet} .'' All citations should then be written in the right order in the list of references at the end of the text, with the writing procedure as illustrated in the example.

\section{Footnotes}

Metode pengembangan yang digunakan adalah Secure Software Development Life Cycle (SSDLC), yakni perluasan dari SDLC yang memasukkan praktik keamanan sejak fase awal sehingga risiko kebocoran data dan biaya perbaikan pascadeploy berkurang signifikan \cite{Riley2024}. \noindent a. Perencanaan (Planning): proses penetapan tujuan dan penentuan langkah untuk mencapainya \cite{Arifudin2021}. Keluaran tahap ini berupa analisis kebutuhan dan rencana proyek terperinci (jadwal, sumber daya, anggaran, dan aktivitas) sebagai peta jalan (roadmap).

\noindent c. Implementasi (Coding/Implementation): penulisan kode sesuai spesifikasi desain untuk menghasilkan kode sumber yang siap diuji.

\noindent d. Pengujian (Testing): pengujian berlapis (unit, integrasi, sistem, UAT) hingga sistem stabil.

\noindent e. Penerapan (Deployment): penerapan ke lingkungan produksi termasuk migrasi data dan konfigurasi server.

\noindent f. Pemeliharaan (Maintenance and Support): perbaikan bug, pembaruan fitur, peningkatan kinerja, dan dukungan teknis berkelanjutan.

\subsubsection{Concurrent Development}

\section{Introduction}
Municipal solid waste remains a critical issue in Indonesia. Waste is a byproduct of human activities that often still carries inherent value yet loses it due to irregular mixtures and compositions \cite{Mahyudin2014}. Under Law of the Republic of Indonesia No. 18 of 2018, waste is an environmental problem that requires special handling. According to the Ministry of Environment and Forestry (KLHK), national waste generation reaches 68.5 million tons per year, with plastic and household waste contributing 11.6 million tons \cite{KLHK2022}. Globally, Indonesia ranks second after China as the largest contributor of plastic waste and is therefore often referred to as a "Plastic Waste Emergency" \cite{Putu2020}. Indonesia produces 24,500 tons of plastic waste per day, or 8.96--9.13 million tons per year \cite{AlamsyahFadli2023}; major challenges remain in achieving efficient and sustainable waste management \cite{KLHK2020}. Plastic waste is inorganic, difficult to degrade, and requires special handling; its prevalence is exacerbated by high population growth and shopping habits that generate single-use plastic bags \cite{Kuswardhani2016}.

Problems and solution alternatives have become increasingly urgent alongside rapid population growth and urbanization \cite{Lingga2024}. One promising solution is the waste bank, a facility for collecting sorted waste while providing economic incentives to communities \cite{Ariefahnoor2020}. Waste banks help reduce the volume of waste at the community level and at final disposal sites (TPA) \cite{Suryani2014}. Social benefits include shifting public perception of waste from valueless to economically valuable, creating new jobs, and encouraging behavioral change in waste management \cite{Ramadani2021}. Sorted waste is delivered to collectors, enabling citizens to obtain monetary or in-kind rewards.

In practice, many waste banks still rely on manual bookkeeping, which is prone to data loss, inaccuracy, and inefficiency. Digitalization is a key solution to improve efficiency, security, and accessibility of transaction records while fostering transparency and real-time data analysis \cite{Radiansyah2022}. We propose an integrated website and mobile application to minimize human error and improve the quality of decision making.

\section{Results and Discussion}
The PPK Ormawa program of the Student Activity Unit for Reasoning and Research (UKMPR) in Muntang Village focuses on digitizing the Bank Sampah Sahabatku to overcome inaccurate manual records, low efficiency, and limited transparency. The intervention strategy uses SSDLC (Iterative and Concurrent) to enable efficient, adaptive, and secure development. Digitalization improves operational efficiency, transparency, accountability, and real-time data accuracy, which increases community participation and sustains environmental education. Implementation includes a website (Filament-based admin panel for administration and real-time transaction monitoring) and a mobile application (Kotlin Native) for transaction logging, balance monitoring, and education. Training and outreach were provided to waste-bank managers and the community to accelerate adoption; pilot testing and early evaluation ensured feature stability and accommodated feedback.

\section{Conclusion}
A web-and-mobile digitalization of waste-bank management using the SSDLC approach in Muntang Village improves efficiency, transparency, and real-time data accuracy. The developed application increases community participation and supports environmental education, achieving an 80\% increase in user registrations and a 90\% reduction in operational time. These outcomes demonstrate the effectiveness of the solution in raising awareness and improving service quality in waste-bank operations.
